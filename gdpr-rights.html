Data protection

Your GDPR rights

YourTurn is committed to handling personal information fairly, transparently and securely. UK data protection law gives individuals a number of rights over their personal data.

Right to be informed

You have the right to clear information about what personal data is collected, why it is used, how long it is retained and who it may be shared with.

Right of access

You can ask for a copy of personal data we hold about you and information about how it is being processed.

Right to rectification

You can ask us to correct inaccurate or incomplete personal information.

Right to erasure

In certain circumstances, you can ask us to delete your personal data. This is sometimes called the right to be forgotten. This right is not absolute and legal exceptions may apply.

Right to restrict processing

In certain circumstances, you can ask us to restrict how your personal data is processed while a concern is considered.

Right to data portability

Where the legal requirements are met, you may request personal data in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and ask for it to be transferred to another service.

Right to object

You can object to certain processing of your personal data, including direct marketing. Whether an objection can stop other processing depends on the lawful basis and circumstances.

Automated decision-making

Where applicable, you have rights relating to decisions made solely by automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects. YourTurn will explain relevant automated decision-making where it is used.

How to make a request

Email support@yourturn.org.uk with enough information for us to identify your request and respond securely. We may need to verify your identity before releasing or changing personal information.

We normally respond to valid UK GDPR rights requests within one month, although applicable law can permit an extension for complex or numerous requests. These rights have exceptions and are not all absolute.

For independent guidance, you can consult the Information Commissioner's Office guidance.
